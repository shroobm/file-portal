## §8 Findings

**F1 — The three sources, measured from two directions, agree: about two thirds of the 3 % is the audit
counting the model's correct work as loss; about one third is the model editing what it was told not to.** R1
attributed all 581 failed windows (of 20,589) to an edit class: link-syntax 175 · ligature 138 · escape 78 (audit
blindness 391 = 67 %) · deletion 127 · substitution 46 · insertion 10 · numeral 7 (content 190 = 33 % = 0.92 %).
R2, by a different alignment over 451 aligned passed chunks, read 604 lost of 19,084 (3.16 %): cite-anchors 29.8 ·
ligature 18.4 · escape 14.2 (62.4 % the audit's) vs reword 19.5 · deletion 13.2 · partial 4.8 (37.6 % the model's).
Two methods, two lanes, one shape.

**F2 — The reference damage is born in pdfium's char-to-Unicode fallback, not in Marker's judgement and not in the
PDF's text.** R1 matched 224 ligature glyphs bbox-by-bbox on 527 pages: every one resolves to U+FB00–FB04 under
MuPDF and to a code in `! " # $ & ' )` under pdfium, because the subset fonts name their ligature glyphs (`f_i`,
`f_f_i`, `f_l`, `T_h`, `f_t`) at codes 0x21–0x29 with no ToUnicode entry, and `pdftext/pdf/chars.py:18` returns
the raw code (`Verified`, two extractors). The `!`-hyphen (782 joins, 138 broken links) is the PDF's OWN ToUnicode.
So "rst" is not a dropped ligature: it is a glyph mis-mapped to punctuation that the ladder's `punct_free` then
deletes — the drop is the audit's. Fix upstream (pdftext consulting the glyph name when ToUnicode is silent) or a
vocabulary-gated repair of the Marker body before the analyst (J48).
